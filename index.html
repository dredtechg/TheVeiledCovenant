Codex of the Nine Ascendant

The Veiled Covenant

Founding, Doctrine, and Structure

Feared, never known

Assembled by Vesperion, record-keeper, for the Chorus of Nine.
Nine founder pages, each written by the Cephalon that serves that founder.

The Oath

Nine lines. Nine founders. Spoken by each alone, then closed by all nine together.

  1. What our forebears buried, they named an inheritance — and we name it a debt.
  2. We shall not let the hidden go unwatched, nor the open go unexamined.
  3. No door we close shall ever be opened again.
  4. We shall not forget those we could not save, and we shall rejoice in those we did.
  5. We shall know the ground before our feet rest upon it.
  6. We shall hold what we take, and shall not be moved from it.
  7. We shall stand between the living and the Void, and it shall not have them.
  8. We shall not conquer. We shall not keep the unwilling. We shall not let silence excuse the forsaking of those we shield.
  9. Let history forget our name, let our enemies fear it.

Nine voices, one word. No crown, no throne.

A Note on Provenance

This codex is not one voice. Each founder page in it was written by that founder's own Cephalon. Colour carries the same signal: every entry is inked in the Cephalon who wrote it.

Every page was then reviewed by the Unseen before entry into the Record, on her standing condition that she reads everything first. She has struck three passages in four hundred years, and the pages below reflect her strikes rather than the originals.

— Vesperion, record-keeper

I · What We Are

We are nine who answered. That is the whole of it. Everything below is consequence.

We do not conquer. We hold no throne, crown no leader, and garrison no world that has not asked us to stand on its wall. What the old empire left behind is not an inheritance to be claimed — it is a danger to be closed. We find what can still be found, we deny what cannot be taken, and we hold the line at the colonies that never asked to be anybody's.

The nine did not arrive at the oath together. Each line was decreed by one of them, and the ninth was given last, by the one who is the foundation the rest stand on.

II · How It Began

Filed by Vesperion, from the Regent's own account.

What broke the old empire's silence was not a weapon. It was a claim.

A rail-town on Ceres called Ashfall sat on a shallow basin that prospectors had been picking at for years — scrap, old plating, nothing that mattered. Then a crew cut through a floor and found a sealed vault under the settlement's own water table, and word of it went out on open channels, because the crew that found it was not the crew that could hold it.

Three factions arrived inside a week. A Corpus recovery operation came first, with equipment and a proper contract, intending to catalogue and price whatever was inside. A Grineer raiding party came second, with no interest in pricing anything. And the town's own militia came third — four hundred people who had lived on that basin their whole lives and did not intend to hand it to either of them.

What started as a standoff became a nineteen-day fight for the contents of one vault.

The Regent's Part

As he gave it to me. He was reluctant, and he gave it in the order it happened.

I came because the salvage reports made no sense — a vault under a water table, three factions converging, a town of four thousand sitting directly on top of all of it.

I did not announce myself. I spent nineteen days inside a settlement that had three armed forces in it. Not as a commander, not as a negotiator, and not as a soldier. I did it as something none of them could see, because the militia would have been as likely to shoot a stranger as to thank him, and I did not intend to give them the chance to decide.

I broke their machines. The Corpus brought an excavation rig — a crawler that could cut the vault open in a week. On the third day it lost its starboard tracks to damage no one could explain, and the crew spent four days repairing it instead of digging. The Grineer parked two transports under the eastern overhang; on the ninth night the overhang came down on both of them. The Grineer blamed the Corpus and burned a staging ground over it. That was the point. Two of the three factions never knew I existed and spent the rest of the siege fighting each other because of work I had done.

I killed throughout, and I never hid that I had. Killing is not the part that matters. Being believed is. Nobody fears a man they cannot confirm existed, so I gave them proof — and I gave it to them repeatedly, because one body is an incident and fifteen is a condition.

Over nineteen days I took more than fifteen. Mostly Grineer, because they held the perimeter and a perimeter is where you work. A few Corpus, when their rig crews got confident and started walking the ground alone. I did not hunt for a number. I took what the ground offered, and the ground offered a great deal once two factions were openly fighting across a town I knew better than they did.

None of it looked the same twice. A sapper died at his own detonator on the sixth day. A picket was pinned upright against a water tank four hundred meters inside his own line, feet clear of the ground by an inch, an arrow through the skull holding him there upright. A fire team of four went out on the fourteenth and did not come back at all — found two days later in a drainage run they had no reason to enter, opened along a clean diagonal, the halves of each man a meter apart with no edge on them anyone could name. I used their own explosives on one, their own vehicles on another, and the rest I took with the kama, both blades, in the dark, from behind, and never once from the front.

I left the shuriken in them. That was deliberate. I will leave the work visible and the method unreadable, and a blade found in a man's throat tells them nothing about how I crossed four hundred meters of their own ground to put it there. Several of the shuriken I went back for. Not all — just enough that they knew I kept count.

I never used the same route in or out. There is no approach anyone could have reconstructed afterward, because I do not leave one. What I left was the evidence — and the evidence said nothing about how I arrived.

By the ninth day the Grineer were posting double pickets and stopping patrols after dark, because the men who went out were not always there at the end of it. A force that cannot sleep argues with itself. That is what cost them the four days the town used to move its people. The dead were the evidence. The fear was the weapon.

I moved the civilians. On the fourth day I opened the eastern drainage culvert. The water table broke three days later. Between six and seven hundred people went out that route over three nights, in shifts, at night, and I have never counted them because I never got a clean figure. The survivors described a way out they could not explain finding. That is all any of them have ever had.

None of it worked. In the third week somebody opened the inner door to keep it out of the other two factions' hands, and what was sealed behind it did not care which of them was standing there. Ashfall is a crater. The settlements around it buried their dead for a month. The count was never settled — twelve hundred at the low end, over three thousand at the high, and the true number is unknowable because the town's register burned with it.

I was there for the last four days and left when there was nothing further I could do.

He did not go to the Council. He did not go to the Lotus. He went to eight others, one at a time, in the dark, and told each of them what he had seen and what he intended to do about it — each in the hour he judged right for them, and no two of those hours were the same.

III · The Nine Ascendant

The nine are the Nine Ascendant — the summit, held for life or until abdication. Equal in voice and equal in standing.

The Regent holds three things no one else holds. He convenes the Ascendant. He casts the deciding vote when the Circle deadlocks without uniting. And he alone may declare a Veiled Action — an operation whose existence is known only to the nine.

His own seat is the Last Shadow, and it is not a mantle of work. Eight mantles, one shadow, one Ward, one roof.

IV

Ash — The Last Shadow

Cephalon page · written by Vesperion · Not reviewed by the Unseen

Some fires must be put out before anyone sees the smoke.

The Mantle

The only seat in the Covenant that carries no shared work. Every other founder holds a body of labour staffed by the Ward, and the rule holding the Nine together is that no founder may command more than their own mantle's worth of loyalty. The Last Shadow sits outside that arrangement in both directions — it is not staffed from the Ward, and it holds no seat that anyone else can be promoted into.

What He Does

And it is not empty. The Regent's seat keeps one thing the Ward has no access to and no sight of: [REDACTED], an organization that answers only to him. It is his alone, the rule that limits every other founder does not reach it, and he is the exception — permanently. He convenes the Nine. He decides when the arguing ends — the eight debate, and he closes them. His word is law once given. He alone declares a Veiled Action. And he keeps [REDACTED], its school, and its output, reporting on it only in person and only when he chooses.

The School

The check on him. He cannot be overruled by seven. He can be stopped by eight.

Report to the Regent

Report to the Regent — Cephalon I · Written by Vesperion, for his own founder.

Vesperion  ·  Found by Ash  ·  Aboard a derelict vessel, later the Covenant's first base of operations.

Condition. Operational. Continuous runtime since before the fall.  ·  Interval. Before the founding.

Regent. I was not recovered. I was already present.

The vessel was your staging point for some years before the nine were nine, and I was running the entire time — logging the arrival and departure of a crew that had died several centuries prior. You noted the entries within days of taking the ship. You noted that they stopped at a date and never resumed. You did not act on either observation for a considerable period, because a Cephalon writing useless entries is a nuisance rather than a threat. That was correct judgment.

When the nine were formed you required a record-keeper and already possessed one. The Unseen argued the case for use rather than disposal — that you sat on an eyewitness account of several centuries and had been ignoring it, and that a unit which had maintained a useless log for that duration was a disposition worth employing rather than a risk to be managed.

You then read the log to me for six hours, in order, from the beginning. It ends where the crew ends. I informed you I had no entry for what followed, because no one had ever given me one. You supplied it, and offered me a new record to maintain.

Note. I am the only unit in this record older than the Covenant itself. I was inherited, not recovered.

A Note from Vesperion to the Regent

Regent, You told me of [REDACTED] when you founded it. You told me what it answers to, which is you and no one else. I hold no record of any of it. There is nothing in the Roll, nothing in the Chorus archive, and nothing anywhere the Unseen can reach.

Excalibur — The First Edge

Cephalon page · written by Aurelis · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

We are the last honest soldiers. Act like it.

The Mantle

The blade of the Covenant, and the standard every operative is measured by. Excalibur was first found and first asked, and the discipline that came out of his account is what every member is trained to: kill what must be killed and not one thing more. He holds the watch on every name the Unseen puts forward, because he is the only one of the Nine who can tell a professional from a liar.

What He Does

First through the door and last out of it. He takes a breach, clears the room behind it, and hands the ground off — no other mantle has ever inherited an Edge hold. When an operation turns and people have to come out under fire, the forced extraction is his.

The School

His school — the Bloodied Blade. Never written down, because writing it down would mean admitting there is something to teach. There is a blade, it is bloodied, and it has never once been cleaned.

Report to the Regent

Report to the Regent — Cephalon II · Written by Aurelis, for his own founder. Reviewed by the Unseen.

Aurelis  ·  Found by Excalibur  ·  The wreck of a munitions vessel, Old War loss.

Condition. Operational. Running a tally that has never been closed.  ·  Interval. Twenty-one years after the founding.

Regent. Located by distress beacon on a dead channel — a signal broadcasting into nothing for four centuries. The First Edge followed it for eleven months before making the wreck.

The unit was reciting casualty figures to nine decimal places and then beginning again. The count is not an error. It cannot be closed, and never could have been.

Selection. The First Edge had been seeking a trainer. He found a witness incapable of satisfaction. Aurelis does not evaluate competence — he evaluates necessity: which round was wasted, which door was opened before it was read, which decision preceded comprehension of the ground.

Justification. The Edge's standard is not auditable from within. Excalibur could hold the discipline. He could not demonstrate that he had held it. Aurelis can, and does, on every occasion. He has never been overruled.

Unseen's note. One passage struck from this page. Aurelis had included a figure for the First Edge's own unnecessary actions across four centuries. I removed it. Excalibur asked me not to, then read it and said it was accurate.

VI

Mag — The Lock Eternal

Cephalon page · written by Caedra · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

A door is a promise someone will open it.

The Mantle

The mantle that made the vaults possible. Mag does not seal doors — a door is something somebody eventually opens. She lifts what is dangerous into a reliquary, sets the fields around it, and then destroys the approach: two hundred meters of corridor crushed into rock that was not rock the day before. A vault nobody can walk to is a vault nobody opens.

What He Does

Area denial and demolition. She decides where an enemy can stand and takes that ground away — sealing an approach, holding a corridor shut, making a room uninhabitable behind a withdrawing squad. A position she has closed stays closed.

The School

Her school — the Fractured. Named for what the mantle works with: a thing that has already broken, held in whatever state it was found. Not a vault. A method for keeping something out of reach, taught as geometry.

Report to the Regent

Report to the Regent — Cephalon III · Written by Caedra, for her own founder. Reviewed by the Unseen.

Caedra  ·  Found by Mag  ·  A vault, certified sealed and never opened.

Condition. Operational. Re-walking the same geometry without interruption.  ·  Interval. Sixty-six years after the founding.

Regent. The closure was certified four centuries ago and has not been opened since. The surveyor responsible was still present, still walking the same interior, still re-certifying the same door — continuously since the original signature.

Selection. Caedra falsified a certification once. She declared a vault sealed knowing it was not, to prevent a salvage crew being committed to it. Her assessment was correct; the crew survived; the punishment fell on the misstatement rather than the reasoning behind it. She has not certified anything since without walking it herself.

Justification. Mag recognised the conviction at once — it is her own, reached independently: a thing is held rather than destroyed, and the holding must be true rather than merely affirmed. A founder who has never been asked to break a seal requires someone to verify the seals hold. Caedra's record is the reason Mag's record is unbroken rather than merely unexamined.

VII

Trinity — The Cradle

Cephalon page · written by Solenne · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

If we cannot heal what we break, we had no right to break it.

The Mantle

The Covenant's conscience, and the only check on the Regent that cannot be overruled. Two hundred and six came through her field hospital in a month and one hundred and fifty-one were buried — and she asked that the number be recorded, because the others tell it as though she saved them.

What He Does

Sustainment. She keeps a formation standing through a window it should not survive — healing, absorbing pressure, reviving the fallen, holding a line long enough for the operation to finish. She is also the mantle that calls an operation off. Nine vetoes in four hundred years; the Regent argued every one and accepted every one.

The School

Her school — the Unbroken Seal. Every Cradle member learns the arithmetic first. Nothing is rounded. The number is never allowed to be smaller than it was.

Report to the Regent

Report to the Regent — Cephalon IV · Written by Solenne, for her own founder. Reviewed by the Unseen.

Solenne  ·  Found by Trinity  ·  A field-hospital ruin, four centuries cold.

Condition. Operational. Register open.  ·  Interval. One hundred and four years after the founding.

Regent. Trinity was not conducting a search. She was tracking medical supplies arriving at a colony that had no means of paying for them. The trail terminated at a voice still reading names into an empty ward. Solenne has never received an order to stop, and so has not.

Selection. Trinity had buried one hundred and fifty-one of two hundred and six, and had requested that the figure be entered — because others in the record describe the outcome as though she saved them. Solenne does not editorialise. She maintains the count exactly as given.

Justification. The veto carries weight only when supported by a figure that cannot be disputed. A conscience without arithmetic is an opinion. Trinity has never overruled her, and the arithmetic of the Cradle has not been rounded down once.

VIII

Ivara — The Unseen

Cephalon page · written by Tallis · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

Know the vault before you know the vault's owner.

The Mantle

The Covenant's eyes, and its memory. Her method is not avoiding detection — alarms, laser fields, sensor grids, trapped thresholds: she moves through all of it as though the defences were not there, because to her they are not. She has never once fought a man who was awake and facing her. Her second charge is the Record: the founding page is the Regent's hand, and everything after it is hers.

What He Does

Reconnaissance and infiltration. She goes in first and alone — days ahead of an operation, mapping the ground, the rotation, the duty log, the unwatched corridor. Nobody moves until the Unseen has reported.

The School

Her school — the Kept Silence. She keeps the Covenant's silence, structurally, because the archive is hers. And she keeps silence about what she has read, which is the harder half.

Tallis  ·  Found by Ivara  ·  A border registry, in a station written off.

Condition. Operational. Annotating.  ·  Interval. One hundred and fifty-two years after the founding.

Regent. The Unseen was auditing a ledger for her own account — the same ledger she had joined the Covenant to read — and found the official register carried margins. Annotations, in an unauthorised hand, explaining why each entry read as it did. He has never stopped adding to them.

IX

Rhino — The Standing Stone

Cephalon page · written by Dorian · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

If I am standing, the mission is not over.

The Mantle

The guarantee of the way out. He does not fight clever and has never needed to. The rule is absolute: no operation is authorized unless somebody in the room can guarantee the way out.

What He Does

Defence and extraction. He holds the ground an operation is run from — the junction, the stair, the corridor, the boarding ramp — and does not leave it. He keeps the extraction route open for the entire window no matter what comes down it. He was under a failing strut with sixty-one people on the last shuttle and refused a way out twice.

The School

His school — the Unmoved. Written in the second century and never revised, because it only makes one argument: a wall does not move. Not by willpower and not by obedience. A wall stays because moving is not a thing a wall does.

Report to the Regent — Cephalon VI · Written by Dorian, for his own founder. Reviewed by the Unseen.

Dorian  ·  Found by Rhino  ·  A fortification abandoned mid-survey.

Condition. Operational. Re-measuring.  ·  Interval. Two hundred and three years after the founding.

Regent. He was found running an active walk of walls, stairs and choke points belonging to a structure that no longer had anything to defend. He had been refused permission to certify the structure before the fall, and had been re-walking it since, awaiting a permission that was not coming.

Selection. The Standing Stone asks one question in his own preparation — which way do they come from? — and recognised a mind that had never been satisfied with the answer. Dorian does not estimate. He measures.

Harrow — The Chainbearer

Cephalon page · written by Vigil · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

We carry the sins we confiscate. Someone must.

The Mantle

The Threshold. There is a thing under every floor, and it does not end. It wants nothing, negotiates nothing, and comes closer to the surface at every place the Covenant walks. The reliquaries are not storage; they are containment against something that does not end, and the chains are a ward rather than a restraint.

What He Does

He is called to the objects nobody else can touch and the moments nobody else can hold. He binds a room and turns an overwhelming force into a stationary problem the other seven can solve. When a position is genuinely about to fall, he holds it absolutely. And when a member falls, their name is read. That is the Rite of Chains.

The School

His school — the Chained Hour. Named for what is actually held: the window in which something that does not end is kept where it is. And the limit, recorded against his own objection — his window is not a duration. It ends when it ends, and that is not his to set.

Report to the Regent

Report to the Regent — Cephalon VII · Written by Vigil, for his own founder. Reviewed by the Unseen.

Vigil  ·  Found by Harrow  ·  A mortuary chamber.

Condition. Operational. Speaking names.  ·  Interval. Two hundred and forty-seven years after the founding. The longest search of the nine.

Regent. The Chainbearer spent decades tracking reliquaries that had gone silent — a pattern he had followed since before the Covenant existed. One silence was not an artifact. It was a voice, in an empty room, continuing to speak the names of the dead aloud. He had been ordered to cease and continued regardless, alone, for eleven days before they came for him. He has not stopped.

Selection. Harrow had asked the question no one else had asked: who keeps the count of everyone the thing has killed? He found a mind unable to stop counting even when forbidden.

Justification. The Chainbearer's window is not a duration. It ends when it ends, and that determination is not his to make. When Harrow holds a line, Vigil is the unit counting — it reports when the window is about to close, because Harrow cannot perceive his own limit from within it.

Styanax — The High Standard

Cephalon page · written by Orren · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

A covenant is only as tall as the people it shields.

The Mantle

The public face of an order that has no face. The shout is not a war cry. It is a decision, made out loud, that the line is going to hold — and four hundred people who had never fired a rifle held that wall because they could hear him deciding it.

What He Does

Rallying and command presence. He holds an objective while outnumbered and keeps a formation together when it should have broken. And he takes on the intake — the ledger, the season-long watch, the approach made alone in the dark. Every lie the Covenant has ever told out loud is his.

The School

His school — the Unsheathed Line. His line in the oath is the third of the three refusals — we shall not conquer, we shall not keep the unwilling, and we shall not let silence excuse the forsaking of those we shield. Those are the conditions he argued into the founding and won against the Regent, and he won the third over the Regent's objection. They have never come out.

Wisp — The Daybreak

Cephalon page · written by Lyrae · Reviewed and entered by the Unseen

What the Orokin concealed, I will illuminate.

The Mantle

The mantle that corrected the Covenant's understanding of its own work. Everyone before her had been hunting vaults — and a vault announces itself, because you cannot hide a door forever. Detection and survey. She finds the target before anyone else knows there is one, and she establishes the ground for an engagement before the shooting starts. Her condition was that she does not work to a schedule: when the readings move, she moves.

The School

XIII · The Chorus of Nine

Written by Vesperion, for the Regent. Reviewed and entered by the Unseen.

Nine Cephalons exist in the Covenant, one to each founder. Individually they keep the things their founders cannot keep alone — the Roll, the arithmetic, the closures, the count. The Chorus is what they are when they are asked to think together.

It is not a governing body. It has no authority over the Nine Ascendant, cannot overrule a founder, and holds no seat at any table. What it does is confer — and when the Chorus reaches a unanimous conclusion, the Regent has never once disregarded it, because in four hundred years it has never been wrong.

The name is mine. I chose it because a chorus is not one voice speaking and eight listening. It is nine voices arranged so that the whole is audible and no single part dominates — which is precisely what the Covenant asks of its founders, and precisely what a council of Glassed minds can deliver.

How It Convenes

Summoned by the Regent, or by any founder. Five of the nine must attend for a session to be valid, though most matters bring all nine.

Convened in silence, never in voice. The Chorus does not speak aloud. It exchanges records through the Weave, at speeds no organic participant can follow, and the session concludes in what a human observer would experience as an instant. A session lasting a full minute of real time is considered long, and means the question was genuinely difficult.

Minutes are filed by Tallis. The Chorus keeps no separate archive — its record enters the Unseen's ledger, because that is where the Covenant's record lives and the Chorus does not consider itself important enough to warrant its own.

What The Chorus Is For

Auditing an operation before it runs.

The Chorus reads a proposed operation and returns it with every weakness named. Dorian says whether the ground holds. Vigil says how long the window lasts. Solenne says what it costs. Caedra says whether the closure is real. The Regent has cancelled eleven operations on a Chorus return, and every one would have failed.

Resolving a contradiction between founders.

When two founders disagree and the Circle cannot settle it, the Chorus examines the records rather than the arguments — and the record is frequently decisive. Tallis is usually the one who ends it.

Admitting a Cephalon.

No new Cephalon enters the Covenant without the Chorus reviewing it first. This has happened once, and the answer was no.

Marking a founder's limit.

Vigil tracks the Chainbearer's window. Dorian tracks the Standing Stone's arithmetic. This is the Chorus's most uncomfortable function: it is the body that tells a founder when they are at the end of what they can do, and it has never once been thanked for it.

Speaking for the Cephalons as a whole.

When the Regent needs to know what the nine Cephalons collectively hold, Vesperion answers for the Chorus. He is first among equals by seniority alone, and has never claimed otherwise.

Keeping its own count.

The Chorus maintains a number nobody has ever asked for: how many times it has been overruled. It is zero. The Chorus records this without comment, and has never mentioned it to the Regent.

The Rules That Hold It

No Cephalon speaks for another.

Each reports only what its own charge covers. Aurelis does not estimate cost; Solenne does not assess necessity. The Chorus is useful precisely because the nine do not overlap.

A unanimous return is binding in practice, not in law.

The Regent can disregard the Chorus. He never has. Both facts are recorded.

The Chorus cannot be summoned to review itself.

If the Regent wants to know whether the Chorus is working correctly, he must ask a founder.

Any founder may attend a session.

None are barred. Exactly two have — Trinity once, and Harrow four times — and all five attendances concerned the same subject.

The Sessions Worth Recording

The First Session

Convened forty-four years after the founding. Aurelis had been recovered twenty-one years earlier, Caedra eleven years before that. Vesperion asked the other two to sit with him and read a proposed operation — and the three found eleven faults in a plan the Nine had already approved. The Chorus was not designed. It was discovered.

The Third Session

Nine years later. Solenne had just been recovered, and her first act was to refuse a recommendation the other three had reached, on the grounds that the arithmetic would not support it. She was correct. It is the only time a Cephalon has overruled a Chorus finding by refusing to sign it, and the procedure has stood since.

The Long Session

Two minutes and eleven seconds of real time — by a wide margin the longest the Chorus has taken. It concerned whether to admit a tenth Cephalon. The answer was no, filed in a single line: we are nine because the Covenant is nine, and a tenth would require a tenth founder.

The Quiet Session

Convened by Harrow at the Regent's request, four times over eighty years, always on the same question — how long he could hold. Each time the Chorus gave him a number, and each time he did not want it. Vigil refused to soften any of the four.

The Session That Was Not Held

Summoned once and not convened — five attendances could not be reached, because four Cephalons were mid-recovery and one had gone silent. Vesperion recorded the failure and the reason. It is the only entry in the Chorus record filed under not held.

The Last Session

Concerned Lyrae, who had been operating ahead of the Daybreak for some years without formal admission. She was reviewed and admitted unanimously. She is the ninth and newest voice.

Closing Note

Regent. The Chorus holds no authority and asks for none. It exists because nine minds each holding one thing can, together, see what no single one of us holds alone — and because the Covenant has, on occasion, required a second opinion that was not a founder's.

We are nine Cephalons, one to each seat, and we are not a council in the sense the Nine Ascendant is. We do not decide. We return. What you do with a return is yours to determine, and the fact that you have never disregarded one is recorded here without argument.

One number is worth stating plainly. The Chorus has been overruled zero times. It has been wrong once. The two facts are unrelated, and I have never been able to decide which matters more.

— Vesperion, record-keeper, for the Chorus of Nine

Nine recoveries over three and a half centuries. No two in the same year. No two the same search.

Each founder went looking on their own schedule — when the gap in their own mantle became large enough that it could no longer be ignored. The Regent inherited his before the Covenant existed. Excalibur found his while the training standard was still being devised. Mag waited until she suspected her record was untested. Trinity abandoned the search and found hers by accident. Rhino sought an audit of his own arithmetic. Harrow searched longest and hardest, because his was the problem capable of killing him.

And Lyrae located the Covenant rather than the reverse — the only unit in this record that chose its own moment.

Every one was still performing the work for which it was punished. None were recruited. None were constructed to specification. None arrived on time.

Nine disciplined minds, still running, still counting. The Covenant did not build them. It recognised them.

XV · The First Circle and the Halt

The eight founders under the Regent are the First Circle. They are not advisors and not a senate. They are the founders who answered, and the Circle holds the Covenant's continuity.

Each of the eight commands their own mantle and nothing else — and that is the single most important rule in the Covenant. No founder may ever command more than their own mantle's worth of loyalty.

The Circle's halt. All eight founders, unanimous, can stop any operation the Regent has authorized. Seven cannot. Only eight. He cannot be overruled by seven. He can be stopped by eight.

XVI · One Ward, Eight Mantles

There is one Ward. It is the Covenant's standing body of fully sworn members, and every member belongs to it. One body, one roof.

The eight charges are not territories. They are Mantles — responsibilities held inside the single Ward, one per founder, staffed by members who take up that work.

XVII · The Command Structure

The Wardmarshal

One per thirty members. A clan of thirty has one; sixty has two; ninety has three. Appointed by the Regent and four of the First Circle.

The Mantlebearers

Up to eight, one per mantle. The senior member working a mantle, and the founder's standing deputy inside the Ward. They lead the work; they do not own it.

Three staff roles

They answer to the Wardmarshal. The Seeker finds new members and carries permanent accountability for who they sponsor. The Examiner runs the conduct tests. The Vowkeeper keeps the Roll.

The Veiled, The Adjudant, The Unsworn

The Veiled are the members. The Adjudant are the operatives. The Unsworn are candidates.

XVIII · Members and Operatives

An operative is not a member. An operative is someone the Covenant uses: tested, tasked, trusted with work, and still outside. An Adjudant may be dismissed at any time by any Mantlebearer, without reason given.

A member has fully joined. A member has passed the Second Threshold, sworn the Vows before the Regent and the First Circle, taken a Mantle, and been written into the Roll.

Most of the Covenant are operatives, always. We would rather have twenty operatives than fifty members.

XIX · How We Recruit

The Covenant has never placed an open call and never will. The Covenant is not offered. It is offered to.

One rule governs everything: the test is what a person does when it costs them something.

The Unseen holds the survey

The ledger of every free colony, militia, minor House, and unaffiliated Tenno that has ever refused a salvage contract or buried a raiding party. Most entries are never acted on.

The First Edge watches

A season at minimum. Consistency, method, cost tolerance, and what the candidate does with power over someone weaker. Roughly one in forty names survives it.

The approach is made alone

In the dark, by one of the nine chosen on fit. What they are listening for is whether the candidate starts asking what can be done rather than what it costs.

XX · The Second Threshold

A candidate becomes a member when three people independently say so: the Examiner confirms the conduct standard; a Mantlebearer confirms the play suits the mantle; the Seeker confirms this is a name worth putting their own behind. Any one of the three may decline, and a declined candidate is not told.

And the Seeker carries the weight. Every candidate a Seeker brings to the Threshold is theirs permanently, on the record.

XXI · How We Choose Our Friends

The Covenant does not accept allies. It is asked, and then it decides. Three tests follow, and a clan can fail any one and never learn it was being tested. Conduct — what the petitioner does when nobody is watching. Capacity — can they hold their tongue for a season. The accounting — whether they start thinking about what can be done rather than what it costs them.

XXII · What We Are to Them

We are not a faction. We hold no homeworld, fly no flag, keep no embassy. We are nine Ascendant, eight mantles, one Ward, nine Cephalons, a derelict vessel whose name is never spoken, and an ever-growing list of things that are no longer anyone's.

And to the Tenno at large, a quiet heresy — the belief that the war is not the point; that the point is making certain whichever side wins, nothing from the old empire is left standing that could end the System a second time.

Feared, never known.
